                    <td width="100%" height="167" class=pTableContent>
                      A monoclonal antibody is a type of antibody produced from a single cell known as a hybridoma that has a single specificity and is of one class or subclass. The production of monoclonal antibody begins with the injection of a mouse with a particular antigen phases. In a few weeks, when the B lymphocytes begin proliferating in response to the antigen, the spleen, a primary source of these lymphocytes, is removed from the mouse and fused in vitro with myeloma cells by adding polyethylene glycol. The hybridoma clones are formed through a three-step process: selection, screening, and cloning. The hybridoma cells are selected by cultivating in HAT supplemented media and screened by ELISA to identify those hybridomas that are producing antibody molecules of the desired specificity. The hybridoma clones can be used to produce a particular antibody in mice (ascities fluid) or tissue culture medium. The total time required to produce a monoclonal antibody in Genesis Biotech Inc. is about 
                      120-180 days.
