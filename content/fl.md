            <br><font size="2"><font color="#FF0000">Fluorescence</font> is a technology that is now used routinely in life science research.&nbsp;Fluorescence reagents are used extensively to trace the presence of biomolecules in cells&nbsp;and other biological systems. The great advancement of fluorescence reagents has promoted&nbsp;a host of more complex fluorescence technologies such as fluorescence resonance energy&nbsp;transfer (FRET).&nbsp;</font>         
            <p><font size="2"><font color="#FF0000">Fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET)</font> is transfer of the excited state energy from&nbsp;the initially excited donor (D) to an acceptor (A). The donor molecules typically emit at&nbsp;shorter wavelengths that overlap with the absorption of acceptors. The process is a&nbsp;distance-dependent interaction between the electronic excited states of two molecules&nbsp;without emission of a photon. FRET is the result of long-range dipole-dipole interactions&nbsp;between the donor and acceptor. Excited donor molecule has several routes to release its&nbsp;captured energy returning to the ground state. The excited state energy can be dissipated&nbsp;to the environment (as light or heat) or transferred directly to a second acceptor molecule,&nbsp;sending the acceptor to an excited state. The latter process is called FRET.             
            </font>
            </p>
            <p><font size="2"><font color="#FF0000">Fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)</font> is an activated precursor used for fluorescein labeling.&nbsp;For efficient N-terminal labeling, a seven-atom aminohexanoyl spacer&nbsp;<br>         
            (NH2-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2-COOH) is inserted between the fluorophore (fluoroscein) and the&nbsp;<br>         
            N-terminus of the peptide. This spacer helps to separate the fluorophore from its point of&nbsp;<br>         
            attachment, potentially reducing the interaction of the fluorophore with the biomolecule to&nbsp;which it is conjugated and making it more&nbsp;accessible to secondary detection reagents.</font><font size="2"><br>
