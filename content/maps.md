<h1 class="page-title">Multiple Antigenic Peptide (MAP)</h1>
      <TABLE cellSpacing=0 cellPadding=0 width=584 border=0 height="84">
        <TBODY>
        <TR>
          <TD valign="top" width="560" height="88" colspan="2">
          <strong><font color="#6699FF">Multiple Antigenic Peptides (MAPs)</font></strong>          
            <br><font size="2"><font color="#6699FF"><b>MAPs</b></font> stands are Multiple Antigenic Peptide, is a branched peptide at which linear&nbsp;peptide chains are linked at their C-terminus via polylysine core, there by increasing&nbsp;the size of whole molecule. 元郁有限公司 provides several choices for MAP such&nbsp;as 4 branches asymmetric or Symmetric, and 8 branches asymmetric or symmetric.Therefore,&nbsp;it can enhance antigen by increasing peptide’s molecular weight. If we choose MAP as&nbsp;antigen, it is unnecessary to conjugate carrier protein..<br>                 
          </font>
       
          </TD>
     
        </TR>
        <TR>
          <TD valign="top" width="560" height="1" colspan="2">
            <br><font size="2"><font color="#FF0000">MAP</font> is simply an alternative to KLH conjugation. This is done to eliminate the coupling&nbsp;of peptides to KLH. It is recommended only for short peptides located either internally&nbsp;within a protein or at the N-terminus.KLH conjugation is recommended for a peptide at&nbsp;any location in a protein and when there is a cysteine residue within the peptide sequence.                  
            </font>
            <p><font size="2">It seems that, however, conformation of peptides on MAP is less flexible, and antibodies&nbsp;obtained by MAP often recognize protein less often than by conventional KLH conjugation.&nbsp;In addition, there is no free peptide produced when making MAP, making it difficult to&nbsp;remove polylysine core directed antibodies. Purification of MAP by HPLC is difficult, and&nbsp;MAP is provided without mass verification due do its heterogeneity and large molecular&nbsp;size.<br>   
            </font>
            </p>

          <TD valign="top" width="339"><strong><font color="#0000FF">Application</font></strong><p>
			<font size="2">Multiple antigen peptide application is one potent 
			way to produce high-titer anti-peptide&nbsp;antibodies and synthetic 
			peptide vaccines. This system utilizes the a- and e-amino groups&nbsp;of 
			lysine to form a backbone to which multiple peptidechains can be 
			attached. Depending on&nbsp;the number of lysine tiers, different numbers 
			of peptide branches can be synthesized.&nbsp;This eliminates the need to 
			conjugate the antigen to a protein carrier.&nbsp;</font></p>
